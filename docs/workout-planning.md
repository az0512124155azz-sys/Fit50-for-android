# Workout selection

The Android app selects a session from the native exercise catalog in `WorkoutSelectionEngine.kt`. This is a conservative exercise-planning rule set, not a diagnosis, medical clearance, or a substitute for individual clinical advice.

1. Normalize questionnaire values and keep unknown answers conservative. Last training history sets a maximum catalog difficulty; pain, movement avoidance, selected conditions and medications lower that ceiling. Reported pain areas exclude exercises whose catalog `avoid` tags overlap. Equipment-specific movements require an explicit equipment answer.
2. Exertional chest pain or a clinician-imposed activity restriction return `clearance_required` with no exercise list. Reported heart disease or surgery also does so unless the user reports clinician approval; approved cases remain at the lowest difficulty. Pain of 8–10 on a typical day instead limits the session to 15 minutes, one set, shorter repetitions/holds and longer rests while excluding movements tagged for the reported pain areas. If filtering cannot produce a warm-up, at least two main exercises and a cooldown, return `no_safe_exercises`. The home and workout pages must not fall back to the static workout in either blocked case.
3. Form a full session with warm-up, main and cooldown slots. Main slots prioritize complementary movement patterns (lower body, upper push/pull, balance, aerobic, core or mobility according to the goal), then fill remaining slots by goal and activity preferences. A movement family appears at most once per session. Push/pull or balance/core emphasis rotates across days; four or more planned sessions a week also cap strength volume at two sets per exercise.
4. A stable hash of user, date, goal and exercise breaks close scores. The same inputs produce the same session on a given day. Exercises completed in the most recent three locally recorded workouts before today get a ranking penalty. Today's completed workout does not change today's plan. This rotates selection; it does not infer that completion means the user is ready for a harder exercise.
5. A long training break or elevated pain limits the plan to one set of each exercise, fewer repetitions or shorter holds and longer rest. No automatic progression beyond the catalog's prescribed difficulty is attempted without feedback on effort and symptoms.

The algorithm uses the existing 66 exercise entries. New catalog entries must receive a movement pattern in `patternFor`; missing classifications fail tests. Library tempo variants retain the pattern and equipment requirement of their base motion. The questionnaire records self-reported clinician approval but cannot verify it; it does not capture exertion, symptom changes during a session or measured ability, so the planner cannot make clinical judgments from those signals.

The session's requested duration is a planning budget, not a claim that the movements will take exactly that many minutes. A clinician or qualified exercise professional should review the exercise tags, exclusion rules and demonstrations before medical use.

The multicomponent design follows the [CDC older-adult activity guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/older-adults.html) and [WHO guidance on balance and strength for older adults](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/336656/9789240015128-eng.pdf). The stop-and-review behavior for exertional chest pain follows [American Heart Association warning-sign guidance](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/cardiac-rehab/getting-physically-active/develop-a-physical-activity-plan-for-you). These sources support the broad safety and variety principles; they do not validate this app's individual exercise prescriptions.
